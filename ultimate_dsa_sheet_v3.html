Questions People Skip But Shouldn't

  • Sum of Subarray Minimums — companies love it, almost no one solves it cleanly. Two monotonic stacks.
  • Continuous Subarray Sum — modulo prefix sum. 10 lines, asked at Google/Amazon constantly.
  • Contiguous Array — replace 0→-1 trick. Appears at Meta frequently.
  • Maximum Profit in Job Scheduling — DP + binary search. Rarely prepared, frequently asked at Amazon senior.
  • Aggressive Cows / Allocate Pages — canonical binary search on answer. Flipkart, Razorpay, Cred OAs.
  • Gas Station — candidates overthink the proof. Simple once you see it.
  • Minimum Platforms — nearly every Indian product-company OA. Almost nobody on LeetCode prepares it.
  • Longest Well-Performing Interval — prefix + mono stack hybrid. Skipped universally. Asked at Google.
  • Number of Ways to Arrive at Destination — Dijkstra + path counting. Missed by almost everyone.
  • Find Eventual Safe States — reverse graph + BFS. Elegant and skipped.
  • Reverse Linked List — yes, really. 40% of candidates stumble on in-place reversal during interviews under pressure.
  • Kth Smallest in Sorted Matrix — appears more than Kth Largest. Both heap and BS approaches required.
  • Subarrays with K Different Integers — atMost(k) - atMost(k-1) trick. Elegant but almost always missed.
  • Redundant Connection — DSU in 10 lines. Good sanity check question. Appears in OAs as a warmup.

Questions That Unlock Multiple Patterns

  • Trapping Rain Water — 3 approaches: two pointers, prefix arrays, mono stack. Know all three.
  • Word Break — DP, BFS on string, trie+DP. Three distinct solutions.
  • Kth Smallest in Sorted Matrix — heap traversal OR binary search on value range.
  • Longest Consecutive Sequence — hashset O(n), DSU, or sorting.
  • Word Search II — backtracking + trie together. Neither alone is enough.
  • Sliding Window Maximum — sliding window + monotonic deque.
  • Maximum XOR of Two Numbers — bitwise greedy with hashset, OR binary trie.
  • Burst Balloons — interval DP that looks nothing like standard DP initially.
  • Find the Duplicate Number — Floyd's cycle detection on an array-as-linked-list.
  • Median of Two Sorted Arrays — binary search + partition + merge concepts all at once.

⬛ Questions Slightly Over-Weighted (Still fine to do, but lower priority)

  • Expression Add Operators (Hard) — Appears at Google very rarely. Very few interview reports. Move to optional.
  • Broken Calculator — Fun thinking question but doesn't appear in recent interview reports. Keep optional.
  • Minimum Jumps to Reach Home — BFS + difference array hybrid. Rarely seen in product company interviews. Optional.

⬛ Common Preparation Mistakes (Verified from Interview Reports)

  • Doing Hard problems before mastering Medium: The #1 mistake. If your Easy solve rate is not 95%+ and Medium is not 75%+, do not touch Hard problems.
  • Pattern-hopping: Doing 2 questions per pattern and moving on. Minimum 8–10 must-do questions per pattern before claiming you "know" the pattern.
  • Not revisiting after 2 weeks: If you solved a question but can't re-solve it from scratch 2 weeks later, it doesn't count. Space repetition matters.
  • Ignoring GFG/non-LeetCode questions: Aggressive Cows, Allocate Pages, Minimum Platforms are must-do for Indian product company OAs and are not on LeetCode proper.
  • Not practising verbal explanation: In real interviews, you must explain your approach while coding. Practice talking through problems out loud from Week 5 onwards.
